The first house on Douglas Avenue

What a $62,000 rental in Wichita taught me about the part of business nobody puts in the spreadsheet.

By Eric Beckman

The closing took eleven minutes. I had read for eight months on BiggerPockets about cash flow, cap rates and the one percent rule, and the whole thing came down to a woman in a title office sliding papers across a desk while a space heater ticked in the corner. Then I owned a house on Douglas Avenue and a tenant I had never met.

The spreadsheet said the house would clear two hundred and ten dollars a month. The spreadsheet did not say that the tenant's son would call me at 9:40 on a Tuesday night because the furnace had quit, or that I would spend the next hour on the phone finding someone to come look at it, or that I would lie awake afterward wondering whether I was the kind of person who could do this.

I was not, yet.

Every owner I have talked to since has a version of that night.

Some of them tell it as a funny story now. Some of them still get quiet. The ones who kept going all describe the same turn: the moment the business stopped being a thing they had bought and became a thing they were responsible for. Nobody describes it as a decision. It happened to them, on a Tuesday, and they answered the phone.

The numbers are the easy part. The work is the phone calls nobody else wants to make.

That is what this site is about. Not the numbers, which anyone can find, but the people who answer the phone, and what they learned by doing it.
